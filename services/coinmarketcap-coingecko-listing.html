What CoinMarketCap and CoinGecko Actually Require

Understanding the requirements is step one. Meeting every requirement in the format reviewers expect is where most projects fail.

CoinMarketCap Listing Requirements

  • Active trading on at least one CMC-supported exchange with verifiable, organic volume
  • Verified smart contract address on a supported blockchain explorer
  • Working project website with clear project information — anonymous or incomplete team pages raise flags
  • Logo, description, and social links formatted to CMC's exact specifications
  • No evidence of wash trading, artificial volume inflation, or coordinated price manipulation
  • Exchange data feed compatible with CMC's API or data partner network

CoinGecko Listing Requirements

  • Minimum transaction activity — at least 10 on-chain transactions in the last 30 days
  • Listed on at least one active DEX or CEX with real, verifiable liquidity
  • Token contract verified on a supported block explorer
  • Live project website and active social media presence
  • Trust score factors evaluated — including exchange reputation, liquidity depth, and project transparency signals

We prepare your submission against every checkpoint on both lists — so reviewers find no grounds for rejection before they find your token.

Why Applications Fail

Why Most Crypto Listing Applications Get Rejected

The platforms do not publish a detailed rejection breakdown. They decline and move on. But after managing 75+ listing engagements, the failure patterns are consistent.

Volume That Does Not Pass Review

CMC and CoinGecko both assess whether trading volume is organic. Wash trading, even small-scale, is identifiable and will trigger rejection. Low legitimate volume below threshold minimums is equally disqualifying. Volume needs to be real, sustained, and verifiable before you apply.

Documentation Submitted in the Wrong Format

Logo dimensions, file formats, description character limits, social link structures — each platform has specifications that are not always obvious. A submission that looks complete to a founder can be technically incomplete to a reviewer scanning against a checklist.

Contract Not Verified or on an Unsupported Explorer

An unverified contract is a trust signal failure at the first checkpoint. Reviewers will not manually verify your contract — they check whether verification exists on a supported block explorer and move on if it does not.

Websites That Raise Compliance Flags

Missing whitepaper, anonymous team with no professional presence, broken links, or website copy that resembles a pump-and-dump promotional page — all of these trigger manual review flags. Your website is reviewed by a human during the listing process.

Premature Submission

Submitting before your token has sufficient trading history, before liquidity is established, or before your social presence signals a legitimate active project almost guarantees rejection. Reapplying too quickly after rejection can also trigger slower review cycles.

We audit every one of these factors before a single submission goes out.
